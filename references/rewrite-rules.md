# CV Rewrite Rules

## Purpose

A CV bullet is a hiring-market argument supported by evidence. It is not a transcript, evidence inventory, or neutral activity log.

## Default construction

Use a compact sequence when evidence permits:

1. Professional capability or scope label.
2. Strong, accurate ownership verb.
3. Business or research context.
4. Method and selected evidence.
5. Scale or metric.
6. Decision, output, or organizational relevance.

Not every bullet needs all six elements. Prioritize the elements that counter the target employer's hiring risk.

## Established user-calibrated rules

- Default to two or three rendered Word lines per bullet. Four full lines are usually too long.
- Put the hardest or most differentiating achievement early. Do not bury recruitment ownership, end-to-end responsibility, or strong metrics.
- Use strong ownership language such as `led`, `owned`, `built`, or `established` when evidence supports it. Do not weaken real responsibility into `supported` or `shared` merely to sound cautious.
- Professional packaging should synthesize the implication of details. Do not simply list every supporting anecdote.
- Use concrete numbers as attention anchors, but do not foreground a nonlinear timeline, career gaps, or duration unless duration itself proves expertise.
- Interview hours, participant counts, survey responses, concepts entering formal requirements, and other comparable scale indicators can serve as professional proof.
- When terminology may be unevenly understood, pair an accessible local-language explanation with a recognized English term in parentheses.
- Every phrase must earn its space by communicating execution maturity, role relevance, market fit, differentiation, or credibility.
- Study the JD and company-stage problem before choosing wording. The same evidence may need a different hiring-market implication for a different role family.
- Preserve useful high-level workplace language when it accurately describes scope, such as feedback loops, alignment, stakeholder management, research infrastructure, or decision visibility.
- Avoid language that accidentally creates a negative signal. Reframe a stopped project as product-definition calibration or investment judgment when that is accurate, without hiding the underlying fact if asked.
- Keep AI claims on the business and workflow side unless engineering evidence exists. Emphasize workflow diagnosis, redesign, orchestration, adoption, and research enablement; do not imply model training, evaluation engineering, or data-science ownership.
- Distinguish an AI-native workflow from AI added as a feature only when the product architecture supports that claim.
- Do not overstate incomplete work. A panel, automation, or business request that is still being established or validated must not be described as a proven operating outcome.
- Company background is not personal impact. Do not claim an OEM-to-brand transition as an individual result.
- Protect the distinction between proposal, formal requirement, implementation, launch, and measured business impact.

## Chinese and English versions

Build both versions from accepted canonical claims. Localize density, terminology, and reader expectations independently. The English version may be more compressed, but it must not drop essential ownership or introduce stronger unsupported claims.

## Audit questions

- What hiring risk does this phrase reduce?
- What evidence ID supports each ownership, scale, and outcome claim?
- Is the strongest signal early enough?
- Is the bullet a synthesis rather than an evidence list?
- Does it create an avoidable negative interpretation?
- Can any word be removed without losing a needed hiring-market signal?
- Does the rendered length fit the assigned budget?
